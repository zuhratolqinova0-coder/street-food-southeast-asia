Globalization And Tourism

Research subpage | Aziza Sultonmurodova

Street food in a globalized world

Globalization connects local food traditions to international tourism, migration, media, trade, and digital platforms. Dishes once associated mainly with a particular neighbourhood or community may become internationally recognized. This wider visibility can create new customers and business opportunities, but it can also change how food is prepared, marketed, and consumed.

Tourism and cultural experience

Food tourism allows visitors to experience a destination through markets, vendors, ingredients, cooking methods, and eating spaces. Jeaheng and Han (2020) studied international tourists' perceptions of Thai street food. Their research identified cultural and local experiences, menu and atmosphere, food quality, value, authenticity, and service among the attributes relevant to tourist behaviour.

Migration and food exchange

Migration carries recipes, techniques, ingredients, and food businesses across borders. Migrant communities may establish stalls and restaurants that introduce dishes to new audiences. At the same time, recipes may be adapted to local ingredient availability, religious requirements, customer expectations, and regulations.

Authenticity and adaptation

Authenticity is often presented as if a dish has one original and unchanging form. In reality, food traditions evolve. Vendors adapt recipes to local tastes, prices, ingredients, and customer demand. The important question is not whether change occurs, but who controls it, who benefits, and whether communities retain the ability to define their own food traditions.

Digital globalization

Social media can make individual vendors famous and allow consumers to discover local markets. Delivery platforms can expand business reach but may also alter packaging, portions, preparation schedules, and customer relationships. Digital visibility is not equally available to all vendors.

Conclusion

Globalization does not simply destroy or preserve street-food culture. It creates new connections and pressures. Sustainable tourism should promote local knowledge, respect vendors, distribute economic benefits fairly, and avoid reducing complex food cultures to tourist spectacles.
