Politics And Government Policies

Research subpage | Sayyora and research team

Why street food is political

Street vending takes place in shared urban spaces. Decisions about permits, sanitation, transport, market design, waste management, and public order determine where vendors can work and which businesses can survive. These are political decisions because they distribute access to space, income, and public resources.

Regulation and uneven enforcement

Regulation can protect consumers by setting standards for hygiene, food handling, licensing, and waste disposal. However, enforcement can also restrict livelihoods when rules are difficult to meet or applied unevenly. Pulliat et al. (2024) examine street-food regulation in Bangkok, Hanoi, Chicago, and Montpellier. Their study shows that formal rules and everyday enforcement do not always operate in the same way, and that vendors' access to space and income can be affected.

Public health and food safety

Food safety is a legitimate public responsibility. The FAO/WHO Codex Regional Code of Hygienic Practice for Street-Vended Foods in Asia (CXC 76R-2017) sets out hygiene guidance intended for vendors, consumers, and relevant authorities. It emphasizes that food safety depends on practices and conditions across preparation, handling, storage, and sale (Codex Alimentarius Commission, 2017). Vendors and consumers need access to clean water, handwashing facilities, safe storage, waste collection, and practical training.

Urban development and public space

Cities may promote street food as a tourism attraction while also limiting vending in areas targeted for redevelopment or traffic management. This creates a policy tension: governments may celebrate culinary heritage but restrict the everyday work of the people who sustain it. Fair policy should consider vendors as participants in urban food systems, not merely as obstacles to modernization.

Policy recommendations

Governments should consult vendor associations before changing rules; provide affordable licensing and training; invest in water, sanitation, and waste facilities; create transparent and consistent enforcement; and identify suitable vending areas near customers and public transport. Policies should protect public health while avoiding unnecessary loss of livelihoods.

Political conclusion

Street-food governance is most effective when it balances consumer safety, inclusive public space, urban planning, cultural heritage, and the economic rights of vendors.
