THE CULTURE AND LANGUAGE OF STREET FOOD IN SOUTHEAST ASIA

More than a meal: street food tells stories about identity, language, work, migration, power, and everyday life. This digital portfolio explores how street food connects communities across Southeast Asia and how these traditions respond to social, economic, and political change.

How does street food in Southeast Asia reflect and shape cultural identity, language, economic opportunities, political relationships, and everyday lifestyles in a globalized world?

Explore the project

Travel through the histories, social meanings, business realities, government policies, languages, and global connections that shape street-food cultures in Southeast Asia. Our case studies focus on Thailand, Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia, and Singapore.

Why this topic matters

Street food is part of everyday life for residents and an important cultural experience for visitors. It provides accessible meals and livelihoods, while also raising questions about food safety, public space, inequality, heritage, and sustainability. Understanding street food therefore requires more than describing dishes: it requires examining the people, institutions, and relationships behind them.

Project statement

We approach street food as a living cultural practice. We recognize the creativity and knowledge of vendors, the diversity of consumers, and the different histories of Southeast Asian societies. We also examine tensions between cultural preservation and commercialization, and between urban regulation and the right to earn a livelihood.
