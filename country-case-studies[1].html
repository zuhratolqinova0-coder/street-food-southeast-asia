Country studies

Comparative approach

The country case studies illustrate how street-food traditions reflect different histories, languages, public policies, and lifestyles. The five selected cases—Thailand, Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia, and Singapore—provide a useful comparison, but they do not represent the full diversity of Southeast Asia.

What we compare

For each country, we consider: (1) examples of widely recognized street foods; (2) historical and cultural influences; (3) language and naming; (4) the role of vendors in everyday life and the local economy; (5) tourism and globalization; and (6) public policy or food-safety questions.

A note on national identity

A dish should not be treated as the sole or definitive symbol of a country. Food traditions vary by region, ethnicity, religion, class, and generation. Our case studies use selected dishes as entry points for discussion rather than as complete representations of national culture.
