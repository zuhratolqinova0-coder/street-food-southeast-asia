Economics And Livelihoods

Research subpage | Research coordination and economic analysis

Street food as a source of livelihood

Street-food vending can provide self-employment and income for individuals and families. Compared with some formal businesses, a small stall may require less initial capital, although costs for equipment, ingredients, transport, rent or fees, licences, and labour can still be significant. Vendors also support wider supply chains involving farmers, wholesalers, transport workers, packaging suppliers, and market operators.

Micro-enterprises and entrepreneurship

Street-food businesses often operate as micro-enterprises. Their success may depend on location, customer relationships, product quality, consistency, pricing, and the vendor's knowledge of local demand. Research on Indonesian street-food micro-enterprises identifies innovation, human capital, social capital, digital marketing, and online payment adoption as factors associated with business performance (Hospitality on the streets, 2025).

Affordability and food access

Street food can offer convenient meals at prices that are accessible to many urban consumers. This role is particularly important for people who work long hours or have limited time and facilities to prepare food. Nevertheless, prices and affordability differ by location, ingredients, inflation, and consumer income.

Economic vulnerability

Vendors may experience uncertain daily sales, rising ingredient costs, weather disruptions, competition, limited credit, and insecure access to selling locations. Because some businesses operate informally, owners may have difficulty accessing insurance, business finance, training, or formal support schemes. Economic opportunity therefore exists alongside insecurity.

Digital platforms and changing markets

Delivery applications, social media, and electronic payments can help vendors reach customers and promote products. They may also create costs, platform dependence, and pressure to meet delivery expectations. The effects differ between vendors: a business with a strong customer base and digital skills may benefit more than a vendor with limited access to technology.

Economic conclusion

Street food contributes to local economies not only through sales but also through employment, supply chains, and tourism. Public support should strengthen vendors' business capabilities without excluding small operators through expensive or inaccessible requirements.
