Indonesia

Country case study

Food examples

Indonesia's street-food culture includes nasi goreng, satay, bakso, martabak, gorengan, and many local and regional foods. The country is geographically and culturally diverse, so street-food traditions differ across islands, cities, and communities.

Everyday food and identity

Street food can connect consumers to familiar flavours and local routines. Vendors may specialize in a dish, develop regular customer relationships, and pass recipes and skills through families or apprenticeships.

Micro-enterprise and innovation

Research on Indonesian street-food micro-enterprises highlights the importance of innovation, human capital, social capital, digital marketing, and online payments in business performance (Hospitality on the streets, 2025). These findings suggest that small vendors are active entrepreneurs who adapt to changing markets.

Digital change

Social media and delivery platforms can help vendors reach customers beyond their immediate neighbourhood. However, digital participation may require smartphones, reliable connectivity, platform fees, packaging, and the ability to manage online orders. Benefits are therefore not guaranteed for every vendor.

Food safety

Indonesia is one of the countries included in the vendor observations of Soon-Sinclair et al. (2026). The study highlights the importance of infrastructure and practical support, including potable water and hygiene facilities.

Key insight

Indonesia demonstrates that street food is both cultural practice and small-scale enterprise. Supporting vendors requires attention to business skills, infrastructure, and the diversity of local food traditions.
