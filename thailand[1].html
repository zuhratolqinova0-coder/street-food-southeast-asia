Thailand

Country case study

Food examples

Thailand's street-food culture includes a wide range of noodle dishes, grilled foods, soups, salads, desserts, and drinks. Pad Thai is internationally recognizable, but it represents only one part of a much wider and regionally diverse food culture.

Food and national identity

Pad Thai provides a particularly useful example of the relationship between food and national identity. Yao et al. (2024) explain how the dish developed through the localization of Chinese kway teow and was promoted during the Phibunsongkhram government (1938–1944) as part of a wider nationalist project. This demonstrates that national dishes can be shaped by policy, public communication, and cultural adaptation.

Tourism

Street food is an important part of Thailand's visitor experience. Jeaheng and Han (2020) found that tourists evaluate Thai street food through several attributes, including cultural experience, atmosphere, food quality, value, and authenticity. Tourism can create demand for vendors, but it can also influence presentation and expectations.

Urban policy and vendors

Street vending in Bangkok illustrates the relationship between public space, urban planning, and livelihoods. Research by Pulliat et al. (2024), which includes Bangkok and Hanoi, shows that regulation and enforcement can affect vendors' access to selling spaces and income.

Key insight

Thailand demonstrates that street food can simultaneously be everyday food, a national symbol, a tourism product, and a subject of urban governance.
